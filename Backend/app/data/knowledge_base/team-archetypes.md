title: Common Team Archetypes — Strategy Notes
tags: general_strategy, team_building

Competitive singles teams are usually built around one of a handful of
recognizable archetypes, and knowing which one a team is going for changes
what "a good team" even means for it.

Balance: a mix of defensive Pokemon (walls/pivots) and a smaller number of
strong attackers, aiming to control the pace of the game with hazards and
pivoting moves (U-turn, Volt Switch) before winning with whichever attacker
gets the cleanest opening. Most tournament-level teams lean balance because
it's the most forgiving archetype against an unpredictable opponent.

Hyper offense: five or six fast, hard-hitting attackers (often including at
least one setup sweeper) with minimal defensive investment, aiming to
overwhelm the opponent before they can stabilize. Hyper offense is
high-variance — it wins fast against slower, less-prepared teams but
struggles badly if its hazard-setter or lead gets removed early.

Stall: almost entirely defensive Pokemon plus residual/status damage
sources (Toxic, Salt Cure, Stealth Rock, weather chip), aiming to out-heal
and out-attrition the opponent over a very long game rather than score
direct knockouts with attacking moves. Stall is rare at the highest levels
of current metagames specifically because Pokemon like Kingambit and
Gholdengo are hard for a purely defensive team to ever actually beat.

Weather/terrain teams: built around a specific field condition (rain, sun,
sand, Electric Terrain, etc.) and Pokemon whose abilities or moves are
specifically boosted by it — high ceiling if the team gets its condition
set up, but vulnerable to a single well-placed removal or a matchup where
the opposing team simply doesn't care about that field condition.

Most real teams are hybrids rather than a pure textbook example of one
archetype — the useful question when evaluating a team isn't "which
archetype box does this fit in" so much as "what is this team's actual win
condition, and does the rest of the team support getting there."
