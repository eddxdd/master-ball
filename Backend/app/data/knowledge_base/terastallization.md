title: Terastallization Basics — Strategy Notes
tags: general_strategy, mechanics, terastallization

Terastallization changes a Pokemon's type to its Tera Type for the rest of
the battle (once per battle, team-wide — only one Pokemon per team can
Terastallize per game) and gives a same-type-attack-bonus boost to any move
matching that new type, even if the move didn't originally get STAB. This
makes Terastallization useful in two very different ways: defensively
(escaping a bad type matchup entirely, e.g. a Water-type Terastallizing to
Steel to stop being weak to Electric) and offensively (picking a Tera Type
that turns a previously-neutral or resisted coverage move into a
same-type-attack-bonus-boosted nuke).

Common defensive use: a Pokemon that would otherwise be walled or heavily
threatened by a specific type Terastallizes into something that resists or
is immune to that type, effectively becoming a completely different
defensive matchup mid-battle. This is most valuable reactively, in response
to what the opponent has already revealed, rather than pre-planned before
the game starts.

Common offensive use: a Pokemon Terastallizes into the type of its best
coverage move specifically to turn that move into a same-type-attack-bonus
hit, often catching the opponent's intended check completely off guard
since the "correct" answer to the Pokemon's original typing no longer
applies once it Terastallizes.

Practical implication for analysis: because any Pokemon can plausibly be
running any Tera Type, a fully "safe" switch-in doesn't really exist until
a Pokemon has already revealed whether (and into what) it Terastallized in
that specific game — this is why cited advice about a specific Tera Type
should be treated as "the most common/likely choice," not a guarantee.
