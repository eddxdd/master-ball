title: Great Tusk — Strategy Notes
species_id: greattusk
tags: pokemon_strategy, OU, ground, fighting

Great Tusk pairs excellent bulk (base 115/131/131 physical stats) with a
Ground/Fighting offensive and defensive profile that's rarely a passive
switch-in for anything. Its signature move, Headlong Rush, hits hard off its
base 131 Attack and drops its own Defense/Special Defense — a real cost, but
one that's easy to justify on a Pokemon this hard to force out in the first
place.

Common role: a bulky offensive pivot that can either lead with Stealth Rock
thanks to its respectable bulk, or sit in the back as a check to the many
Steel- and Rock-types that define the Ground/Fighting metagame, threatening
them back with strong neutral or super-effective coverage rather than just
tanking a hit and doing nothing.

Checks and counters: Flying-types immune to Ground (and specifically ones
that resist or are neutral to Fighting, like Corviknight) wall it defensively
if it lacks coverage for them. Fairy-types resist Fighting and can often
threaten back with a strong special attack, since Great Tusk's investment is
almost always physical.

Common teammates: A dedicated special attacker or wallbreaker that pressures
the Flying- and Fairy-type answers Great Tusk can't touch cleanly, and a
Steel- or Water-resist teammate that appreciates Great Tusk drawing in and
threatening the Rock- and Steel-types that would otherwise wall a fragile
special sweeper.
